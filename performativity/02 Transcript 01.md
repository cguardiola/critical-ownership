The Greek term **ποιησις** (poiesis) means **"making" or "creation"**—literally the act of bringing something into being or producing something.

The word comes from the verb **ποιειν** (poiein), "to make" or "to create." In ancient Greek thought, it referred especially to creative or productive activity, particularly in the arts.

A few key philosophical distinctions:

**Poiesis vs. Praxis**: Aristotle famously distinguished between:
- **Poiesis**: Production or making, where the goal (end) is external to the activity itself—you make something and then it exists independently of you (like building a house or writing a poem)
- **Praxis**: Action or doing, where the goal is internal to the activity itself—you do something for its own sake (like virtuous living or playing music for enjoyment)

**In Philosophy and Aesthetics**: The concept has been influential in later philosophy. For example, Heidegger used "poiesis" to describe a fundamental way humans engage with the world through creative revelation and unconcealment.

The term lives on in English words like **"poetry"** (the art of making/creating language) and **"poetic"** (relating to creation or imaginative making).
